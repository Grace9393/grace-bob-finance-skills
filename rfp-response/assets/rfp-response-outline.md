# RFP response outline

**Override this whenever the RFP prescribes a structure.** Mirror their numbering exactly — an
evaluator scoring section by section should never have to hunt for your answer. Use this skeleton
only where the client leaves the structure open, or to check nothing is missing from theirs.

Word budget assumes a 40-page limit; scale proportionally.

---

## 0. Cover, contents, compliance declaration
Cover per their format. Contents mapped to *their* section numbers. Compliance declaration and any
mandatory forms — check these against the mechanics list before anything else.

## 1. Executive summary *(2–3 pages, written last)*
About them, not us. Four moves:
1. **Their situation and what is at stake** — in their words, showing you understood the brief.
2. **What we will do** — the approach in five or six sentences, phased.
3. **Why us** — the three to five win themes, each with its proof.
4. **What it is worth** — the quantified outcome, with the basis stated.

No company history. No capability catalogue. If the evaluator reads only this, they should be able
to explain your bid to a colleague.

## 2. Understanding of the requirement *(2–3 pages)*
Demonstrate you understand the problem behind the requirement, not just the requirement. Where an
assessment has been done, this is where its findings earn their place. State the constraints and
sensitivities you have understood — evaluators notice when a bidder has grasped the hard part.

## 3. Credentials and experience *(3–4 pages)*
Relevant, not exhaustive. Per reference: client context, what we did, measured outcome, why it is
comparable to theirs. Cleared references only, with the metric each one proves. One relevant,
evidenced reference beats five logos.

## 4. Approach and methodology *(8–12 pages — usually the highest-weighted section)*
- **4.1** Overall approach and phasing, with a diagram.
- **4.2** Phase-by-phase: activities, inputs, outputs, client involvement, duration.
- **4.3** Deliverables — a table of every deliverable, its format, and its acceptance criteria.
- **4.4** Tools, accelerators and assets we bring; where AI or automation is used and where a human
  decision point sits.
- **4.5** Ways of working — location, cadence, governance, escalation.
- **4.6** Knowledge transfer and how the client's capability is left stronger.
- **4.7** Quality assurance and how deliverables are reviewed before they reach them.

Every requirement in the compliance matrix maps to a subsection here or to an annex. Show the map.

## 5. Team *(2–3 pages)*
Structure diagram, named key roles with allocation percentages and availability dates, one-paragraph
biographies focused on relevance, and how continuity is protected through the engagement. Named,
committed people outscore role descriptions with CVs in an annex.

## 6. Plan, timeline and dependencies *(2 pages)*
Gantt with phases, milestones, decision gates and deliverable dates. **Client dependencies stated
explicitly** — data, access, SMEs, decisions — each with a date and the consequence of delay. This
is the section that protects the delivery team later; do not soften it to look accommodating.

## 7. Risk management *(1–2 pages)*
Six to ten real risks with likelihood, impact, mitigation and owner. Include the risks they will
be worrying about — availability of their people, data quality, change resistance, competing
programmes. A response with no risks reads as inexperienced.

## 8. Commercial *(per their format)*
Price in exactly the structure and currency requested. Pricing narrative: what drives the price,
what is included, where they can flex it. Assumptions as a numbered annex, referenced from the
price table. Validity period. Any commercial options (capped T&M, gainshare) presented as options
with their conditions, not as hedges.

## 9. Terms and conditions response
Compliance position on their terms, with any deviations listed clearly and an alternative offered
for each. Review with the `contract-review` skill (07) from the supplier position before
submission. Never bury a deviation — an unflagged one discovered at contracting costs more than
the point being defended.

## 10. Annexes
Compliance matrix · CVs · certificates and insurance · reference detail · assumptions ·
sample deliverables · any mandatory forms.

## Pre-submission checklist

- [ ] Every mandatory requirement answered; compliance matrix shows zero open
- [ ] Their section numbering mirrored exactly
- [ ] Page, font and format limits met
- [ ] All mandatory forms signed and attached
- [ ] Pricing in the required format and currency; assumptions annexed
- [ ] Every claim carries evidence; every benefit carries a number and its basis
- [ ] No other client's name anywhere in the document
- [ ] No tracked changes, comments or draft watermarks left in
- [ ] All `[SENIOR REVIEW]` markers resolved
- [ ] References cleared for citation
- [ ] Correct portal/address; deadline and timezone confirmed
- [ ] Submitted early, confirmation captured
