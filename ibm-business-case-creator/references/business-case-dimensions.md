# Business Case Dimensions

Use this reference when the case is ambiguous, discovery-heavy, politically sensitive, or missing core information. It is a question bank for scoping and gap analysis, not a required output format.

## How to Use

- Start with the top-level dimensions
- Expand only the areas that matter for the current case
- Do not reproduce the full tree unless the user explicitly asks for decomposition output
- Convert unanswered items into assumptions, risks, or questions for the client

## 1. Strategic Problem

- What business problem or opportunity must be clearly articulated?
- What is the current-state pain, inefficiency, or underperformance?
- What is the cost of inaction or delay?
- What desired future state is the client trying to reach?
- What makes the problem urgent now?

## 2. Options and Decision Logic

- What realistic options exist, including do nothing?
- What lower-cost or lower-scope alternative should be considered?
- What evaluation criteria matter most: value, speed, risk, fit, feasibility?
- Why is the preferred option better than the alternatives?

## 3. Financial Justification

- What total investment is required?
- What benefits are quantified and when do they arrive?
- What payback, cash flow, NPV, or IRR profile supports the case?
- What assumptions and sensitivities control the outcome?
- What downside scenarios and mitigations exist?

## 4. Evidence and Credibility

- What proof points show the solution is viable?
- What case studies, pilots, or benchmarks support the claims?
- What credentials, references, or third-party validation build trust?
- What delivery, commercial, or contractual protections reduce perceived risk?

## 5. Stakeholder Alignment

- Who decides and what do they care about?
- What objections, skepticism, or political friction must be addressed?
- What governance path must the case pass through?
- What change impacts or adoption requirements matter?

## 6. Delivery and Benefits Realization

- What phased roadmap makes delivery credible?
- What quick wins or pilot steps reduce risk?
- What risks, dependencies, and controls matter most?
- What success metrics, baselines, and targets define success?
- Who owns measurement and benefits realization after go-live?

## Signs a Dimension Needs Expansion

- Numbers are missing or inconsistent
- The recommendation feels pre-decided
- Stakeholder objections are easy to predict but not answered
- Delivery is described vaguely
- Benefits are asserted but not owned or measured
